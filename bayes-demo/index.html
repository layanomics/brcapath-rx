in Breast Cancer Subtype Prediction

How observing a patient's receptor status and tumor grade updates our estimate of molecular subtype — computed from real cohort data.

What is Subtype Prediction?

The clinical question this demo addresses

Breast cancer is not one disease. Molecular subtypes — Luminal A, Luminal B, HER2-enriched, Basal-like, and others — differ in prognosis, treatment response, and survival outcomes. Subtype is ideally determined by gene expression profiling, but clinical features such as receptor status and tumor grade are often the first evidence available.

The Question Given what we observe about a patient today — ER, PR, HER2, and grade — what is the probability that this patient has the Luminal A subtype?
Why Bayes? Bayes theorem gives us a principled way to combine a population-level baseline (the prior) with patient-specific evidence (the likelihood) to produce an updated probability (the posterior).

Bayes Theorem — Applied Here

From abstract formula to this exact application

P(H | D) = P(D | H) · P(H) / P(D)
Abstract form
P(Luminal A  |  ER+, PR+, HER2−, low grade)
= ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
Σs P(ER+, PR+, HER2−, low grade | subtypes) · P(subtypes)
Applied to subtype prediction — summing over all 6 METABRIC subtypes

The denominator normalizes over all subtypes so that posterior probabilities sum to 1. Each feature (ER, PR, HER2, grade) is treated as conditionally independent given subtype — a naive Bayes assumption that works well as an approximation here.

The Three Components

Defined in the context of this application

Prior
P(Luminal A)
The baseline probability of Luminal A before seeing any patient-specific evidence. Derived from subtype frequencies in the METABRIC cohort (n = 1,974 patients with confirmed subtype).

In METABRIC: 35.5% of patients are Luminal A.
Likelihood
P(evidence | Luminal A)
How probable is the observed combination of ER, PR, HER2, and grade if the patient truly has Luminal A?

Also computed for each other subtype — LumB, HER2-enriched, Basal-like, etc. The contrast between subtypes drives the update.
Posterior
P(Luminal A | evidence)
The updated probability of Luminal A after incorporating the patient's specific features. Ranges from near 0 to near 1 depending on the evidence pattern.

This is the clinically actionable quantity.

Data Provenance

What is real-data-derived vs. illustrative

All values in this demo are real-data-derived. Priors and likelihoods were computed directly from the METABRIC clinical dataset (n = 2,509 patients; n = 1,974 with confirmed CLAUDIN subtype label), part of the BRCAPath-Rx research project.

Source file: 01-data/processed/metabric/metabric_clinical_clean.tsv  (processed during Phase 1 preprocessing).

The posterior computation uses the naive Bayes assumption: features (ER, PR, HER2, grade) are treated as conditionally independent given subtype. This is a standard approximation; in practice, some correlation exists (e.g. ER and PR tend to co-occur). The demo is intended as an educational illustration of Bayesian reasoning, not a clinical decision tool.
